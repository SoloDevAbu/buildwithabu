## Why raw transcription isn't enough

Speech-to-text writes what it heard. Product names, Hinglish and numbers often come back wrong, and fixing them by hand means editing timing data too.

## Keep timing and text apart

Treat the two as separate jobs:

1. **Timing.** Run [faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper) over the voice track with word timestamps. It runs locally with no API key.
2. **Text.** Use the exact script as the source of truth.
3. **Align.** Match your script's words to Whisper's words, then copy each start and end time across.

The output is a simple list:

```json
{ "word": "Watch", "start": 0.0, "end": 0.14 }
```

## Check the match rate

The share of script words matched to spoken words tells you whether alignment worked. In Reel Studio Kit, anything under 70% usually means the caption text differs from the speech, or that the wrong language was set.

## Hindi and Hinglish

Set the language when you transcribe. Getting it wrong is a common cause of poor matches. For Hinglish, try it both ways on a short clip first, because the best setting depends on how much of the speech is English.

## Make the timing work harder

Once each word has a time, captions are only the start. You can trigger a card change, stamp or cut on a spoken word, which is what makes a reel feel edited.
